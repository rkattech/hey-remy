# The Remy blog voice (calibrated 2026-08-22 against the founder's own paragraphs)

The founder wrote three paragraphs (the opening of the Fired Assistant Test post)
as the reference. THAT prose is the register. The five external references
(waitbutwhy, Manson, Lenny, Koe, Vellum) informed the study; the founder's own
sample overrides them all.

## What his register does (extracted mechanically, follow these)

1. SEQUENTIAL REASONING. Each sentence follows from the previous as an actual
   thought. No jump-cuts for effect.
2. QUESTIONS GET ANSWERED, honestly, often as enumeration: "Sometimes it is
   from references... Sometimes the person just seems honest. Sometimes it's a
   leap of faith." Never a rhetorical question left hanging as a device.
3. PLAY IT FORWARD to concrete ends: "you might even call the bank and change
   your credit card number." Follow implications to the real-world detail.
4. PAYOFFS ARE DISTINCTIONS, NOT PUNCHLINES: "the ability to wow has partly to
   do with model capability and partly the orchestration; the moral standard has
   everything to do with verifiable transparency." An analytical split is the
   satisfying ending. Never a zinger.
5. PLAIN CONNECTIVES: "Yet", "Ultimately", "It turns out that", "So", "And".
6. MEDIUM SENTENCES, few fragments, almost no one-liners. Density of crafted
   punch lines: ~zero. (My drafts had 5 per 800 words. That is the slop.)
7. TRADE-OFFS STATED AS TRADE-OFFS: name the cost of our own choice ("a little
   slower. We think that is the right trade").
8. No coining ceremony in prose. A concept can be the TITLE (Fired Assistant
   Test) but the body never struts "Call it X."
9. Honest limits printed in first person ("I recognize that is a weak proof").
10. His literal phrases are load-bearing; keep them: "care and respect",
    "gets the job done every single time", "teeny bit slower", "needs of the
    many versus the needs of the few", "as much a safeguard of your data as you
    are yourself".
